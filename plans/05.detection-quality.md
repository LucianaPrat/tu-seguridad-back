# 05 — Detection Quality: tu-seguridad-back

> Why cameras that are polling correctly still raise no alerts, what was measured on 2026-09-02,
> and the decisions that follow. Audience: the repo owner (for the optics/provider calls) and an
> AI coding agent or developer (for T01–T06).
> Each task has a **Definition of Done (DoD)**: exact commands and observable results. A task is
> complete only when every DoD item passes.

## 1. Context

The reported symptom: "no person has been detected for a while, and the events are not being
sent". A concrete instance was handed over — a person stood outside camera 08 from roughly
19:24:45 to 19:25:30 local and no alert was raised, while the log showed only

```
[18:27:53.744] INFO: camera 499484a5-6b03-41e4-b197-9d6c05e0c404 now polling detection every 5s
```

Everything downstream of the detector was ruled out by measurement, not by reading:

| Suspect | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Polling stopped | Alive | `pipeline_poll_total{status="success"}` advanced 2205 → 2207 → 2209 across two 45 s samples; 82 polls per camera in the 20 min after the 19:23 restart, one error total |
| Recorder capture failing | Healthy | `dvr_capture_total{outcome="success"}` ≈ 2190 per channel against 1 error per channel |
| Alert cooldown swallowing alerts | Not involved | `pipeline_alerts_suppressed_total` has no series at all — zero suppressions since boot, despite `ALERT_COOLDOWN_SECONDS=300` |
| Occupancy hysteresis mis-wired | Correct | The 19:33:00 alert (below) confirmed an entry through the normal two-poll path |
| Alert persistence / delivery broken | Working | Event `4bcbfedb` on camera 08, `confidence 0.667`, `event_deliveries.status = sent` at 19:33:00.897 |
| **Upstream detector recall** | **Root cause** | §2 |

The `detection` cadence line is not an alert. `levelFor` (`src/modules/pipeline/cadence.engine.ts`)
returns `detection` as soon as an area leaves `Outside`, which happens on the *first* frame with an
anchor inside it. With `ENTER_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS=2` the entry is only confirmed if the next frame,
one `POLLING_DETECTION_SECONDS` later, also sees somebody. A single-frame sighting produces exactly
what was reported: a cadence line and no event.

## 2. What was measured

Method: capture frames straight off the recorder over ISAPI digest, post each one to
`POST {FACE_AUTH_API_URL}/api/v1/persons` with a session token from `/api/v1/auth/authorize`, and
record the raw `persons[]` the upstream returns — before any of our filtering. Ground truth for
every frame was established by eye. 35 images in the paced run plus a 33-frame live sample, zero
upstream errors. `CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD` as shipped is the compile-time constant
`PipelineDefaults.CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD = 0.45` (there is no env override; only the nullable
per-camera `confidence_threshold` column).

### 2.1 Recall, by set

| Set | Ground truth | Detected ≥ 0.45 | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence frames, camera 08 (patio, subject near, daylight) | person | 6/8 | **75%** |
| Evidence frames, camera 04 (street, subject far) | person | 0/8 | **0%** |
| Evidence frames, camera 03 (street/garden, subject far) | person | 0/3 | **0%** |
| Raw night frames, camera 08 | person | 1/3 | 33% |
| Live 33-frame sample, camera 08 at night, subject in view | person | 1/33 | **3%** |
| Current live frames, empty scenes | empty | 0/5 | **0% false positives** |

Every detection landed in `0.514 … 0.779`. Nothing scored higher than 0.779, and — this is the
load-bearing detail — **every failure returned `persons: []`, an empty array, never a low score.**

The evidence-frame set is a lower bound rather than a clean replay: the stored frame is the
annotated re-encode (`annotate-frame.ts`, q88, no resize, outline-only box), because the raw bytes
are discarded (the `ponytail:` note at `annotate-frame.ts:21`). The pipeline had detected a person
on the raw version moments earlier, which is why the event exists at all.

### 2.2 The endpoint is deterministic

| Image | Runs | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Camera 08, daylight, subject at the door | 4 | `0.625` every time |
| Camera 04, daylight, subject on the sidewalk | 4 | `0` every time |
| Camera 08, night, subject facing the door light | 2 | `0.558` both times |

Identical bytes give identical scores to the third decimal. The frame-to-frame flapping is
therefore a property of the images, not flakiness in the service — which also means retrying the
same frame is worthless.

### 2.3 Degradation ladder, on the frame it scores best (0.625)

| Variant | Score |
|---|---|
| baseline | 0.625 |
| brightness ×0.70 | 0.610 |
| brightness ×0.50 | 0.606 |
| blur σ=1 | 0.620 |
| blur σ=2 | 0.593 |
| downscale to 352×288 | 0.626 |
| **grayscale** | **0** |
| grayscale ×0.99 / ×0.85 / grayscale+blur σ=1 | **0** (all three) |
| JPEG q15 | **0** |
| grayscale ×0.50 + blur σ=1 | 0.533 |

Robust to darkness, blur and halved resolution — it barely moves the score. Fragile to colour
removal and to heavy JPEG artefacts. All variants were verified 3-channel sRGB, so the grayscale
collapse is not a pixel-format rejection. The last row contradicts the four above it, which is the
real finding: **the decision margin on these frames is thin and not monotonic in image quality**,
so near-identical frames flip between a detection and nothing.

### 2.4 Upscaling does not rescue a distant subject

Camera 04's frame at 2× and 3× (704 → 1408 → 2112 px wide, q90): still `0` at both sizes. There is
no minimum-pixel-size floor to climb over; the model does not fire on those figures at all. For
scale: in that frame the subject is ~40×100 px in 704×576, roughly 7% of frame width, and the one
detection it ever produced scored 0.50.

### 2.5 The upstream rate limit is real, and the circuit breaker cannot see it

At 250 ms spacing the endpoint answered `429` after ~17 requests and then held a penalty window —
observed backoffs of 15–45 s. At 12 s spacing, 35 consecutive requests drew zero 429s. The
pipeline's own steady rate (5 cameras on a 15 s rung ≈ one detect every 3 s) never tripped it, and
the README already warns about this at `README.md:178`.

The exposure: `mapUpstreamError` folds a `429` into a plain `UPSTREAM_ERROR`
(`src/cross/errors/upstream-error.ts:38`), that throw happens inside the opossum breaker, and the
breaker opens at `errorThresholdPercentage: 50` with `resetTimeout: 30000`
(`face-auth-client.service.ts:52-58`). Nothing anywhere reads `Retry-After`. So a throttle burst —
from a backfill script, a second environment sharing the egress IP, or a provider tightening the
limit — degrades into 30-second windows where **every** camera's detection short-circuits, reported
as an upstream outage.

## 3. Root cause

The binding constraint is the upstream detector's recall, not this codebase. Concretely:

1. **The street cameras are effectively blind.** 0 detections in 11 frames where a person was
   demonstrably present, at ~7% of frame width. Not a threshold problem: the response is empty.
2. **The patio camera works in colour and mostly fails in IR.** 75% by daylight, 3% over a
   33-frame night sample, and the single night hit is the one frame where the subject faced the
   doorway light. Losing colour costs the margin the ladder in §2.3 measures.
3. **Our hysteresis then multiplies the loss.** `ENTER_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS=2` needs two *consecutive*
   hits. At a 3% per-frame hit rate the probability of two in a row is ~0.1%, so at night that
   camera cannot alert by construction — which is exactly the 19:24:45–19:25:30 report.

## 4. Decisions

Each decision names what was rejected and the measurement that rejects it.

**D1 — Do not tune the confidence threshold, in either direction.** Failures are empty arrays, so
there is nothing for a lower threshold to admit. Raising it is worse than useless: the weakest true
positive observed is 0.514, six hundredths above the current 0.45. The nullable per-camera
`confidence_threshold` column stays as it is, unused.

**D2 — Do not add upscaling, sharpening or any pre-processing in the pipeline.** §2.4 shows 2× and
3× change nothing, and §2.3 shows the model is already insensitive to blur and downscale. Image
pre-processing here would be code that cannot help, on the hot path, forever.

**D3 — Do not lower `ENTER_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS` to 1 globally.** It buys the least where it costs the
most: on the street cameras there are no isolated detections to promote (0/11), while every camera
loses the flicker protection that keeps a single bad frame from mailing somebody. Adopt instead a
**K-of-N sliding window** (T03), defaulting to 2-of-4: it tolerates a dropped frame — the actual
failure mode — while still refusing a lone frame. On camera 08 at night, 2-of-4 raises the
per-window confirmation probability from ~0.1% to ~0.5%; that is honest arithmetic, not a fix, and
it is the reason D6 is the decision that matters.

**D4 — Instrument the detector before changing it.** This diagnosis cost a 40-minute ad-hoc probe
because nothing in the process records "the detector was asked and answered nothing". A counter
(T01) plus a committed bench script (T02) turn every future instance of this question into one
query. This is the enabling decision: T03 and D6 are both unverifiable without it.

**D5 — Keep the raw frame for alert evidence, behind a default-off flag.** Re-running history
against the provider is currently a lower bound because only the annotated re-encode survives.
Storing raw bytes for alert frames only (never for live thumbnails), gated by
`SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RAW=false`, is a few lines and the retention sweep from plan 04 T05 already prunes
snapshots. Not on by default: it doubles the blob volume of the highest-volume table.

**D6 — Optics and lighting are the fix, and they are the owner's, not the code's.** No software
change makes 0/11 into a working detector. The two levers the measurements point at are framing
(the subject must occupy far more of the frame on cameras 03 and 04) and keeping camera 08 out of
IR at night. Everything in §5 is either measurement for this or damage control around it.

**D7 — Treat `429` as its own failure class.** T04. A throttle response is not an outage and must
not open a breaker that stops detection for every camera.

**D8 — Escalate to the face-auth provider with the numbers, not with an impression.** §2 is the
message; §6 lists the questions.

**D9 — `monitorMode` and drawn zones must stop contradicting each other.** Cameras 03, 04 and 05
are `monitorMode = full` while carrying 1, 1 and 2 monitor zones. In `full` mode
`PipelineService.resolveZones` ignores them and alerts on the whole frame, so those zones are
decoration that reads like configuration. Owner picks per camera: switch to `partial` or delete the
zones (T05).

## 5. Tasks

### T01 — Count what the detector answers

`pipeline_persons_detected_total{cameraId, outcome}` with `outcome` one of `persons`, `empty`,
`filtered` (upstream returned detections, all below threshold). Incremented in
`PipelineService.processImage` right after the `detectPersons` result is unpacked. Register it in
`src/cross/metrics/metrics.module.ts` next to `PIPELINE_ALERTS_SUPPRESSED_TOTAL`, name in
`MetricNames`.

Also promote one line out of debug: a camera whose area leaves `Outside` but never confirms is the
signal this whole plan is about, and at `LOG_LEVEL=info` it is currently invisible.

**DoD**
- `curl -s localhost:3000/metrics | grep pipeline_persons_detected_total` shows all three outcomes
  after the process has polled every camera for one cadence cycle.
- Unit test in `pipeline.service.spec.ts` asserts the `empty` and `filtered` branches increment
  distinct label sets.
- `README.md` metrics table and `ARCHITECTURE.md` list the metric.

### T02 — Commit the bench as a script

`scripts/detection-bench.ts`, run with `npx tsx`, no Nest boot. Takes a directory of JPEGs or a
recorder channel plus a frame count, posts each frame to the detector, prints one TSV row per image
(`label`, `persons`, `kept`, `scores`) and a summary. Hard-codes a floor of 5 s between requests
(§2.5) and retries a `429` once after 20 s.

Keep it under ~150 lines and do not generalise it: this is the tool that answers "did the optics
change help", nothing more.

**DoD**
- `npx tsx scripts/detection-bench.ts --dir <path>` reproduces the §2.1 table on the same images.
- A run of ≥30 frames completes with zero 429s.
- `docs/BEST_PRACTICES.md` gains a short "measuring detection recall" entry pointing at it.

### T03 — K-of-N entry confirmation

Replace the consecutive counter in `OccupancyEngine` with a bounded per-area history: confirm
`ENTER` when K of the last N frames saw an anchor inside. New env pair
`ENTER_HITS_REQUIRED` / `ENTER_WINDOW_POLLS` (Joi, integers, `ENTER_HITS_REQUIRED ≤
ENTER_WINDOW_POLLS`), defaults `2` / `4`. `EXIT_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS` stays exactly as it is — a false
exit costs nothing, and hysteresis on the way out is what stops duplicate alerts.

`ENTER_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS` is superseded; remove it in the same commit rather than leaving two knobs
that disagree, and say so in the README env table.

**DoD**
- `occupancy.engine.spec.ts` covers: 2-of-4 with a gap confirms; 1-of-4 does not; the window slides
  (a hit older than N frames stops counting); `ENTER_HITS_REQUIRED = ENTER_WINDOW_POLLS` behaves
  exactly like the old consecutive rule.
- Boot refuses `ENTER_HITS_REQUIRED > ENTER_WINDOW_POLLS`.
- `README.md` §detection-pipeline and the env table describe the window, and no reference to
  `ENTER_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS` survives `grep -r`.

### T04 — A throttle is not an outage

In `FaceAuthClientService`: map a `429` to a distinct `ErrorCode.UPSTREAM_THROTTLED`, do not let it
count toward the breaker, and honour `Retry-After` by parking further detect calls until it
elapses. A parked poll re-arms the camera at its current level, the same as any other failed poll —
`pollOnce` already does this, so no scheduler change should be needed.

**DoD**
- Unit test: a `429` with `Retry-After: 5` leaves `circuitState === 'closed'` and the next call
  short-circuits without an HTTP request.
- Unit test: ten consecutive `429`s do not open the breaker; ten `500`s still do.
- The camera's `lastErrorCode` reports the throttle distinctly in `GET /cameras/:id/status`.

### T05 — Stop the zones from lying

For each of cameras 03, 04, 05: apply the owner's answer from D9. If a camera goes `partial`, its
alerts start depending on the drawn area, so re-check it against `GET /cameras/:id/status` before
closing the task. If the zones go, delete them through the API so the audit trail is normal.

**DoD**
- No enabled camera has both `monitorMode = full` and a non-deleted `monitor_zones` row (single SQL
  check, recorded in the task tracker).

### T06 — Keep the raw evidence frame, off by default

`SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RAW` (Joi boolean, default `false`). When on, `storeEvidence` stores the raw bytes
alongside the annotated frame and links the event to the annotated one exactly as today. Never
applies to live thumbnails. Delete the now-stale `ponytail:` note at `annotate-frame.ts:21` in the
same commit.

**DoD**
- With the flag off, snapshot row count per alert is unchanged (e2e).
- With the flag on, an alert produces two rows and `GET /snapshots/:id` still answers the annotated
  one for the event.
- The retention sweep prunes both.

## 6. Owner steps (no code)

1. **Reframe cameras 03 and 04.** A subject at ~7% of frame width is not detectable by this model
   at any post-processing. Either narrow the field of view onto the approach path, or accept that
   these two are recording cameras and not alerting ones — and say so in the product, because a
   camera that silently never alerts is worse than one labelled as passive.
2. **Light the camera 08 patio** so it stays in colour after dark. §2.3 is the argument: the same
   frame drops from 0.625 to zero on colour removal alone.
3. **Camera 07's lens is blurred or obstructed** — its frames are a grey smear. It will never
   detect anything. Clean, refocus or disable it.
4. **Escalate to the provider** with §2 attached, and ask specifically:
   - model and version behind `POST /api/v1/persons`, and its expected recall for subjects under
     10% of frame width;
   - whether monochrome/IR input is supported, or whether a separate model/weights exist for it;
   - whether a minimum-subject-size or sensitivity parameter is exposed;
   - the documented rate limit and whether `Retry-After` is returned (we observed 429 at 250 ms
     spacing and clean runs at 12 s).
5. **Re-measure after each change** with T02 on the same image sets. The acceptance bar for a
   reframe: ≥80% recall on 20 frames of a person walking the approach path in daylight, and ≥50% at
   night, measured, not estimated.

## 7. Explicitly not doing

- **No second detector, no local model, no ONNX sidecar.** Single-host PM2 deployment (plan 02 §1,
  plan 04 §1) has not changed, and adding a model runtime is a new operational surface for a
  problem the provider may fix with a parameter. Revisit only after §6.4 comes back.
- **No motion-detection fallback.** A pixel-difference trigger on eight channels raises alerts on
  headlights, rain and the dogs already visible in the night frames. It would replace "misses
  people" with "cries wolf", which is the failure that gets alerting switched off.
- **No frame retry against the same image.** §2.2 — deterministic, so a retry returns the same
  nothing.
- **No queue, no Redis, no Docker.** Unchanged from plans 02 and 04.

## 8. Appendix

### 8.1 Configuration at the time of measurement

`POLLING_ENABLED=true`, `POLLING_PASSIVE_SECONDS=15`, `POLLING_ACTIVE_SECONDS=10`,
`POLLING_DETECTION_SECONDS=5`, `POLLING_CONCURRENCY=4`, `ENTER_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS=2`,
`EXIT_CONSECUTIVE_POLLS=3`, `ALERT_COOLDOWN_SECONDS=300`, `SNAPSHOT_LIVE_WRITE_SECONDS=300`,
`LOG_LEVEL=info`. No `CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD` env var exists; the global default is the 0.45 constant.

Measured effective cadence on the passive rung: **~22 s**, not 15 s — the 15 s deadline is set at
the *end* of a poll (~1 s) and the next 5 s tick has to come round. Worth knowing before anyone
tunes the rungs against wall-clock expectations.

Enabled cameras: `03` (21b274e6, street/garden), `04` (499484a5, street), `05` (6fce9055),
`07` (6c9007c5, lens blurred), `08` (b542e035, patio). Channels 1, 2 and 6 are disabled.

### 8.2 The live night sample

33 frames pulled from channel 8 every ~3 s between 19:30:26 and 19:32:23 while a person stood in
the patio, one detection: `19:32:19`, `detScore 0.5584352016448975`, anchor `(0.481, 0.539)`. The
frames at 19:31:37, 19:32:12, 19:32:16 and 19:32:18 were inspected by eye and all four contain the
person; only the last scored. Counting only from the first visually confirmed frame the rate is
1/14 rather than 1/33 — both are far below what two consecutive hits need.

Immediately after, at **19:33:00**, the running pipeline raised event `4bcbfedb` on the same camera
at `confidence 0.667` with the email delivered 149 ms later — the subject was facing the camera
under the doorway light. The pipeline confirms an entry correctly the moment the detector
cooperates, which is the cleanest evidence that §3 is where the problem lives.

### 8.3 Reproducing §2

The probe scripts used for this measurement live outside the repo (session scratchpad); T02 is what
makes them reproducible. Until then, the shape is: read `dvrs.password_encrypted` and decrypt with
`DVR_PASSWORD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` (aes-256-gcm, `iv:authTag:ciphertext`, base64 parts), pull
`/ISAPI/Streaming/channels/{port}01/picture?snapShotImageType=JPEG` over digest, exchange
`FACE_AUTH_CLIENT_TOKEN` at `/api/v1/auth/authorize` for the `Fa-Token`, then post each frame as
multipart `file`. Keep ≥5 s between detect calls.
