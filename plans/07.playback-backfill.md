# 07 — Playback Backfill and the Recall Ledger: tu-seguridad-back

> Why one frame per event is the wrong sample, what the recorder can hand back instead, and what to
> keep so the detector can be improved rather than guessed at. Verified against the real DVR on
> 2026-09-09.
> Each task has a **Definition of Done (DoD)**: exact commands and observable results.

## Context

[`plans/06`](06.dvr-event-push.md) shipped and PR #97 is green: the recorder now says when something moved, and that drives a
capture. It still analyses **one frame per event**, taken live, _after_ the notification arrives.

Two separate problems with that, and the recorder holds the material for both — it records
continuously and **at least three weeks deep** (verified: channel 8 has continuous segments back to
2026-08-18, ~1 GB per 2–6 h).

The earlier draft of this plan proposed pulling ~40 frames per event and scoring each online. **That
design does not survive the measured throttle.** This revision keeps the idea, replaces the delivery
mechanism, and puts a cheap decisive experiment in front of any production code.

The underlying problem is still the one from [`plans/05`](05.detection-quality.md): **0 of 11** detections at medium distance,
**1 of 33** on a night scene. This plan is about giving that detector more and better frames, and
about keeping the evidence needed to improve it.

## 2. The constraint that reshapes everything

Two measured facts kill an online per-event burst:

1. **The budget is 1 request per second, shared across everything.** Not per camera, not per
   feature — one bucket for the whole process. Forty frames is forty seconds of the entire estate's
   detection capacity. A backfill paced at the full rate leaves _nothing_ for a live capture.
2. **The park is global.** `throttledUntil` is one field on a singleton, so a 429 provoked by a
   backfill on camera 4 short-circuits live detection on _every_ camera for 5 s — and only ~17 rapid
   requests provoke it. A backfill racing a real intruder would blind the thing that matters.

There is also no concurrency bound on the client at all; a new caller is unbounded by construction.

**Therefore: backfill runs in a reserved slow lane, never at the full rate and never ahead of a live
capture.** At ~0.5 req/s it costs 80 s for forty frames and leaves half the budget free. The repo
already has the deferred-work pattern — `delivery-retry.service.ts` uses a table as the queue plus a
cron, its header recording that BullMQ was declined on cost.

**A corollary worth acting on immediately:** `scripts/try-detect.ts` defaults to a 12 s gap, chosen
when the limit was unknown. Against a stated 1 req/s it is 12x too slow, and it is the tool T00
depends on. Lower its default and keep a floor with margin.

## T00 — Settle the core conjecture. No production code.

**The plan rests on a curve nobody has measured.** Measure it first, with what already exists.

`scripts/try-detect.ts` **already** accepts a directory, scores every image through the real
`FaceAuthClientService`, and summarises frames answered, hit count and percentage, and `detScore`
range. Lower its 12 s default first (see the corollary above) — at 1 req/s a 1.5-2 s gap has ample
margin, and it turns a 40-frame event from eight minutes into about a minute.

Per sampled event:

1. Pick events from history — three weeks are available, so this needs no new events and no waiting.
2. `ContentMgmt/search` the camera's track for `[T − 20 s, T + 10 s]`, take the `playbackURI`.
3. `ffmpeg -rtsp_transport tcp -i "<uri>" -vf fps=2 -frames:v 60 out/%03d.jpg`, on a developer
   machine, not production.
4. `npx ts-node scripts/try-detect.ts out/`, keep the TSV.

**Outputs that decide the plan:** hit rate at 1/5/10/20/40 frames; hit rate **by offset from the
event**; wall-clock per event (expect ~40-80 s for 40 frames, not the 8 minutes the earlier draft
assumed).

**Decision rule, fixed in advance so the result cannot be rationalised afterwards:** if 20 frames do
not at least **double** the single-frame hit rate, T02 is not built and this plan stops at T01.

**DoD** — ≥10 events across ≥2 cameras, night and day; TSVs and the curve committed under `docs/`;
the decision recorded either way.

**Done, 2026-09-10.** Eleven events, three cameras, day and night. Twenty frames took detection from
**2/10 events to 7/10** — 3.5× against a rule asking for 2× — so **T02 is built**. Forty frames added
no events over twenty, and the pre-event advantage the pilot reported did not survive the sample.
Result and raw data: [`docs/T00-frame-window-measurement.md`](../docs/T00-frame-window-measurement.md),
`docs/data/T00-ch*.tsv`.

### T00a — Find the historical motion index (timeboxed)

T00 needs to know _when_ to look. Even at 1 req/s, scanning three weeks of five channels is out of
the question, so the sampling has to be pointed at moments where something happened. `ContentMgmt/logSearch` refused four body shapes; try the Hikvision SDK's documented

independent of publication.

## T01 — The recall ledger

Worth doing regardless of T00's outcome. Every event-triggered capture where the recorder reported
motion and the detector reported **nobody** is a labelled miss, free — and today the frames are
discarded, because `processImage` only persists a frame when there is an alert
(`pipeline.service.ts:164-170`).

The signal exists and is computed in exactly one place: `detectionOutcome(reported, kept)`
(`pipeline.service.ts:322-330`) returns `'empty'` when the upstream reported nothing at all. Its own
doc comment calls that "the upstream reporting nothing … out of our hands" — precisely the ledger's
criterion. **`'filtered'` must not be included**: that is our threshold rejecting a sighting, a
different problem with a different fix.

**Implementation**

- `AnalysisResult` (`analysis-result.ts:12`) carries no pre-filter count. Add what the caller needs.
- Store the untouched frame when the outcome is `empty` and the poll was event-triggered, behind its
  own switch. Follow `SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RAW` (`pipeline.service.ts:267-283`): an audit copy whose failure
  is logged and dropped, because it must never cost an alert. Reuse
  `snapshotService.store(spaceId, cameraId, image)`.

**The retention trap.** The sweep deletes on `{ isLive: false, capturedAt: { lt: before } }` alone
(`snapshot.accessor.ts:132`) — age only, no class column, no reference check. A ledger row is pruned
automatically at `RETENTION_SNAPSHOT_DAYS` (90) **and is indistinguishable from alert evidence and
from `SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RAW` copies**, so it cannot be listed or given its own lifetime.

**Add a nullable `reason` column to `snapshots`.** One migration; makes the ledger listable and
re-runnable, which is the only reason it exists. Inference (an unreferenced `isLive: false` row) is
the no-migration alternative and cannot tell a ledger row from a raw copy.

**DoD** — an event-triggered poll answered `empty` stores its frame with a reason; `filtered` stores
nothing; rows are listable for a date range and feed `scripts/try-detect.ts` directly; retention still
prunes them; a metric counts them; the switch defaults off.

## T02 — Backfill as queued batch work _(gated on T00)_

- **T02a — Size it.** Tee the alertStream to a file for a night; count motion events per camera per
  hour and burst lengths. This is the missing denominator for every cost estimate here, and it also
  settles `DVR_EVENTS_DEBOUNCE_SECONDS`, still a guess.
- **T02b — The window.** The recorder reports no boundaries, so derive them: the burst's first and
  last `active` pulse bracket the movement, minus the ~6 s lag. Requires the listener to **count**
  suppressed pulses rather than drop them.
- **T02c — The queue.** A row per pending backfill, a cron draining it slowly at the measured safe
  spacing, **suspended whenever a live capture is in flight**. Model on `delivery-retry.service.ts`.

**A backfill hit never raises an alert.** It feeds the ledger and the evidence trail only. An intruder
alert five minutes late arrives after it could be acted on and trains operators to ignore alerts;
reporting recall we did not deliver in time would be lying to ourselves about the product.

**Two risks to resolve before writing code:**

- **ffmpeg would be the first process spawn in this codebase.** No `child_process` anywhere in `src/`
  or `scripts/`, no Dockerfile, no provisioning script, no verified inventory of the production host —
  and `docs/decisions/002-hls-live-streaming.md:111` already rejected ffmpeg once. Verify presence on
  the host and write a decision doc, or find a path without it.
- **Park priority.** Split the park into two lanes so batch work can never block a live capture. This
  is correct regardless of T02 and could ship with T01.

## T03 — Ask face-auth for a batch endpoint

face-auth is a sister team with an existing channel — [`docs/face-auth-detection-findings.md`](../docs/face-auth-detection-findings.md) is
already addressed to them.

**Ask for a batch endpoint: N images in one request.** With the limit now known to be 1 req/s, forty
frames cost forty seconds of the _whole estate's_ budget — survivable, but it means a burst of
analysis and live alerting cannot both have the capacity at once. A batch call turns forty requests
into one and removes the contention entirely, which a raised per-second limit would only move around.

Write it as a second findings document, in the same shape as the first: what we measured, what we
need, and why. Include T00's curve — it is the argument. Note that the ask is _also_ in their
interest: a client sending 40 spaced requests is worse for them than one batch.

**Status 2026-09-09:** document written — [`docs/face-auth-batch-endpoint-request.md`](../docs/face-auth-batch-endpoint-request.md) — sent, and
face-auth reported they are building it.

**What that changes on our side.** The slow lane in T02c exists because forty frames were forty
requests against a one-per-second budget. A batch call makes them one, so the queue mostly dissolves
and the backfill can run online. Two consequences to plan for, not to build yet:

- `FaceAuthClientService` gains a batch method beside `detectPersons`. Do not write it against an
  unshipped contract — wait for the endpoint, keep the single-image path working.
- If they ship `stopOnFirstDetection`, the scan loop moves to their side and ours gets simpler.

**And it raises the priority of T00.** They are investing in a batch endpoint on the strength of our
conjecture that N frames beat one. We still have not measured it. If the curve turns out flat we owe
them that finding before they ship, not after.

**DoD** — the answer recorded here; T02 redesigned against the real contract once it exists.

`human` alone is accepted and persists. **Do not set it yet**: the classifier is the first of two
filters, and a person it fails to classify produces no event at all.

## T05 — Alert on the silent degradation

Carried from [`docs/DVR_EVENTS_FOLLOWUP.md`](../docs/DVR_EVENTS_FOLLOWUP.md). With the passive rung at five minutes, a channel that
stops publishing degrades quietly — the recorder keeps heartbeating, the watchdog stays happy, and
`dvr_event_motion_total` flatlining per channel is the only signal. This should exist before anyone
relies on event-driven capture.

## Decisions taken, and what would reverse them

| Decision                                                    | Reversed if                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Measure before building                                     | Never — but argue the decision rule before the run, not after                                          |
| Backfill runs in a reserved slow lane at part of the budget | face-auth ships a batch endpoint (T03), or raises the per-IP limit enough that contention disappears   |
| A backfill hit feeds the ledger, never raises an alert      | The product decides late alerts are wanted, explicitly                                                 |
| Add a `reason` column rather than infer the ledger          | The migration is refused; then accept an unlistable ledger                                             |
| Do not narrow `targetType` yet                              | T02a shows vehicle events dominate volume                                                              |
| Recorder stays on continuous recording                      | Never for this purpose — `EMR` gives exact segments but loses the pre-event frames, which is the point |

## Verification

**T00** is the verification of the premise; its artefact is the TSV plus the curve, committed under
`docs/`. No test suite.

**T01**, per repo conventions:

- Unit (`new Service(mock as never)`, mutable config record): `empty` + event-triggered → one store
  with the reason; `filtered` → no store; `persons` → evidence path unchanged; a failed ledger write
  is logged and never propagates; switch off → nothing stored.
- Integration (`*.int-spec.ts`, real test database): a ledger row is listable for a date range, and
  the retention sweep prunes it.
- Migration: `npx prisma migrate dev`, and confirm existing rows get `reason = null`.

**Gate on every commit**: `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm test`;
`npm run test:all` on the whole change; regenerate `openapi.json` if any DTO changes. Note for local
e2e: `assistant.e2e-spec` fails unless `ASSISTANT_ENABLED=false` — a local `.env` artefact, not a
regression.

**End to end** against the real recorder: turn the ledger on, walk past a camera at night until the
detector misses, confirm a row lands with the reason set, export the frame, and confirm
`scripts/try-detect.ts` reproduces the miss on it.

## Out of scope, deliberately

- Video analysis. Frames go to the same detector one at a time; nothing here learns to track.
- Replacing the live capture. The fast path stays fast.
- Making the recorder the detector. It says _something moved and it looked human_; whether that is a
  person inside a monitored area stays the pipeline's call.
