# 07 — Playback Backfill and the Recall Ledger: tu-seguridad-back

> Why one frame per event is the wrong sample, what the recorder can hand back instead, and what to
> keep so the detector can be improved rather than guessed at. Verified against the real DVR on
> 2026-09-09. Audience: the repo owner and whoever implements T01–T04.

## 1. Context

[`plans/06`](06.dvr-event-push.md) made the recorder say when something moved. It still analyses
**one frame** per event, taken live, after the notification arrives. Both halves of that are wrong,
and the recorder already holds the fix: it records continuously.

### The event is late, and it is late by seconds

Measured on channel 8. The recorder published `VMD active` at **23:11:27**. Pulling its own recording
for the twenty seconds around that moment and extracting frames shows the subject already fully in
frame, centred, at **23:11:21** — six seconds earlier, and a visibly better detection candidate than
anything the live capture could have taken at 23:11:28.

The notification fires when the recorder's classifier is convinced, not when the person appeared. A
live snapshot can only ever see what is left by then.

### One frame is a 3% sample

`plans/05` §2 measured the upstream detector at roughly **one detection per 33 frames** on an IR night
scene. Against that rate:

| Frames analysed | P(at least one detection) |
| --------------- | ------------------------- |
| 1 (today)       | 3%                        |
| 10              | 26%                       |
| 40              | 71%                       |

The recording runs at 10 fps, so a twenty-second window is 200 frames and sampling one in five gives 40.

**Treat that table as an upper bound.** It assumes independent trials and frames are not independent
— a subject at a bad angle stays at a bad angle for many frames. The gain is still large for a
different reason: across twenty seconds the subject _moves_, changing distance, angle and lighting,
which is exactly the variation a single frame cannot provide.

### What was verified, end to end

```
POST /ISAPI/ContentMgmt/search   { trackID: 801, timeSpan }
  -> <playbackURI>rtsp://…/Streaming/tracks/801/?starttime=…&endtime=…</playbackURI>

ffmpeg -rtsp_transport tcp -i "<playbackURI with credentials>" -vf fps=2 -frames:v 40 …
  -> 40 JPEG frames, 960x1088, from the twenty seconds around a real event
```

- Tracks are `<channel>01`; `DefaultRecordingMode` is `CMR` on an all-week schedule, so the recording
  is continuous, not motion-gated.
- `GET /ISAPI/ContentMgmt/record/tracks` reports the retention actually available per track.
- ffmpeg 6.1.1 is present on the development machine. **Its presence on the deployment host is not
  verified and is a gate on T02.**

## 2. What to build

### T01 — The recall ledger (do this first)

Every event-triggered capture where the recorder reported motion and the detector reported nobody is
a labelled miss, free. Today that pair is countable — `dvr_event_motion_total{outcome="triggered"}`
against `pipeline_persons_detected_total{outcome="empty"}` — but the **frames are discarded**, so
nothing can be re-run against a better detector or a tuned threshold later.

Keep the frame when the recorder disagreed with the detector. `SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RAW` already exists for
exactly this reason ("on when the detector's own recall is under audit") and the retention sweep
already prunes snapshots, so this is a condition on an existing write, not a new table.

**Why first:** it is small, and without it there is no way to tell whether T02 helped.

**DoD** — an event-triggered poll returning zero persons stores its frame marked as a recorder/detector
disagreement; the retention sweep prunes them; a metric counts them; the rows can be listed for a
night and re-run through `scripts/try-detect.ts`.

### T02a — Ask the recorder for the window, do not invent it

The obvious objection to picking a window ourselves is that the recorder must already know when the
motion started and ended, since its own app shows a clip per event. **It does not.** Two independent
checks say so:

- All three motion metadata descriptors on `ContentMgmt/search` return the **single continuous block**
  (`recordType.meta.hikvision.com/timing`, 22:39 -> 23:20), never per-event segments. Every schedule
  action on the track reads `CMR`, continuous, seven days, and no `preRecordTimeSeconds` or
  `postRecordTimeSeconds` is exposed.
- The app's clip is a **fixed 2 minutes 10 seconds from the event timestamp**, every time. Constant
  padding, not measured boundaries.

There are no motion segments because nothing is segmenting: the recorder records everything.
Switching it to event recording (`EMR`) would produce discrete segments — and would be a **downgrade
for this purpose**, because continuous recording is what allows pulling the six seconds _before_ the
notification, which is where the best frame was found.

Copying the app's 2m10s is also wrong: at 10 fps that is roughly 1 300 frames for one event.

So the window is ours to choose, and the recorder does report
the duration, in a different form: **it repeats `VMD active` for as long as the motion lasts.** The
first pulse and the last pulse of a burst bracket the movement. The window is
`first pulse - classifier lag` to `last pulse + margin`, with the lag measured at roughly six seconds
on this box.

This is also why the debounce must **count** the pulses it suppresses rather than merely dropping
them: the burst is the only measurement of how long the motion lasted, and T02 needs it.

**DoD** — the listener records first-pulse and last-pulse times per camera per burst, and the backfill
window is computed from them; a single-pulse event falls back to a configured minimum window.

### T02 — Playback backfill on a miss

When an event-triggered capture finds nobody, go back and look properly.

1. Live capture first, exactly as today. Unchanged, and still the fast path for an alert.
2. Zero persons → queue a backfill for that camera and that event time.
3. `ContentMgmt/search` for the window T02a derived, on the camera's track, take the `playbackURI`.
4. ffmpeg pulls it and extracts a frame every N, capped.
5. Detect **serially**, stopping at the first hit. A hit becomes the alert, carrying the frame's own
   timestamp rather than the event's.

**The cost is the risk, and it is a burst.** Forty frames is forty detection POSTs against an
IP-throttled upstream, arriving together. Thirty events per camera per night is 1 200 calls — still
below the 2 880 the old poll spent, but the old spend was evenly spaced and this is not.
`UPSTREAM_THROTTLED` stops being an exceptional path and becomes the hot one. So: serial, never
`Promise.all`; lower priority than any live capture; a per-camera cap on backfills in flight; and
stop at the first hit rather than scoring every frame.

**DoD** — a miss on a camera with recording available produces frames from before the event; a hit
inside the window raises the alert with the frame's timestamp; a recorder with no recording for that
span degrades to today's behaviour; the backfill can never delay or displace a live capture; and the
whole thing is behind its own switch, default off.

### T03 — Decide `targetType` with data

The recorder classifies at the edge and accepts `human`, `vehicle`, or both — verified, and `human`
alone persists. Narrowing looks like an obvious noise cut and is not: the classifier is the **first of
two filters** in front of the detector, and a person it fails to classify produces no event, so that
frame is never looked at at all.

**DoD** — a night of event counts per channel by type, then a decision per camera. Not before.

### T04 — Alert on the silent degradation

Carried over from [`docs/DVR_EVENTS_FOLLOWUP.md`](../docs/DVR_EVENTS_FOLLOWUP.md): with the passive
rung at five minutes, a channel that stops publishing degrades quietly. `dvr_event_motion_total`
flatlining per channel is the only signal.

## 3. What this deliberately does not do

- **It does not analyse video.** Frames go to the same detector, one at a time. Nothing in this repo
  learns to track, and the upstream contract does not change.
- **It does not replace the live capture.** The fast path stays fast; the backfill is a second look
  that only runs when the first one found nothing.
- **It does not turn the recorder into the detector.** The recorder says _something moved and it
  looked human_; whether it is a person inside a monitored area is still the pipeline's call.
